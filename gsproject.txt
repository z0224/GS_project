Description

Gaussian Splatting creates impressive, photorealistic 3D reconstructions from multiple viewpoints. However, these reconstructions are fundamentally visual—they lack an inherent spatial understanding or interaction models. This project seeks to extend Gaussian Splatting into a walkable, immersive virtual experience by aligning reconstructions with real-world geometry and enabling continuous walking-based navigation in virtual reality (VR).

The system will recreate outdoor environments—like streets or campuses—by utilising images taken with a mobile phone, accompanied by latitude, longitude, and heading information. This metadata allows the system to align the reconstructed scenes with real-world geometry, such as the outlines of buildings and pathways from OpenStreetMap (OSM). While this geometry won't be visible to the user, it acts as an invisible navigation framework to establish walkable areas and spatial limitations.

The VR experience will mimic the actual scale and layout of the environment, allowing users to transition smoothly from point A to point B, similar to how they would in reality. The navigable paths will be guided by both reconstructed splat data and reliable external references such as OpenStreetMap (OSM). This strategy contrasts with teleportation-style navigation by focusing on continuous, restricted movement using VR walking methods (like joystick movement or room-scale movement, based on the setup).

Key research question:
Can outdoor Gaussian Splatting reconstructions be aligned with known geographic data to support continuous, realistic walkable navigation in VR environments?
